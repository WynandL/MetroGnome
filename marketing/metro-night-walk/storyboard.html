Metro's Night Walk

At dusk the forest is out of time. Metro steps out with his baton and walks from the creek to the lake to the clearing, and at every stop one more voice falls onto the beat, until the whole forest is a band and the band is in your pocket.

The score is the edit

The film is built like the app: on a click. At 120 BPM a bar is exactly two seconds, so every shot is a whole number of bars and every cut lands on a downbeat. The music is additive. Each place Metro visits adds one layer, which is the whole story told in sound, and it still reads with the sound off because each layer has its own light in the picture.

Character and look

Keep Metro the same in every shot

The look is your existing 3D Metro placed in a photoreal forest: real moss, wet rock, felt and firelight, with a shallow depth of field. The character stays stylised so he matches the store icon and the site; the realism comes from the world and the light. Paste this in place of [METRO] in each prompt:

Metro, a small cheerful garden gnome conductor in a stylised 3D animated film look: a tall bright red felt cone hat with a round brim, square gold-framed sunglasses with dark lenses, a big white handlebar moustache, pointed ears, white hair at the temples, a black pinstripe suit with three gold buttons and a gold belt buckle, a white collar with a red bow tie, glossy red shoes with white soles, and a slim gold conductor's baton in his right hand
Music brief

One tune that builds itself

  • 120 BPM, 4/4, C major, exactly 15 bars. Locking the tempo is what lets every cut sit on a bar line. Ask for a hard ending on the last beat, no fade.
  • The click is the app's Classic click, recorded straight from the app, running from bar 3 to the final frame. It is the one sound in the film a user will hear again when they open the app.
  • Additive arrangement, one layer per place, as in the chart: bass, then flute and kalimba, then guitar, then log drums, then everyone.
  • Acoustic and woody, played small: pizzicato, kalimba, nylon guitar, hand drums. Playful folk, closer to a woodland band than an orchestra.
  • Three sync points the composer must hit: the two baton taps (bar 2, beats 3 and 4), the C, E, G plucks (bar 7, beats 1 to 3), the final chord (bar 13, beat 1).
  • Rights: the track has to be cleared for paid advertising, which many free libraries and some AI music plans do not cover. Check the licence before the edit, since the picture is cut to it.
Making it

Production route

  1. Music first. Get the 30 second track made or licensed and lay it on the timeline with a marker every 2 seconds. Everything is cut to those markers.
  2. One still per shot. Generate a key frame for each shot from the character reference image plus the shot prompt, and keep going until Metro matches across all seven. This is where consistency is won.
  3. Animate each still with an image to video tool. Every shot is 4 or 6 seconds, which suits the clip lengths those tools produce. Generate a second or two extra and trim to the bar.
  4. Shot 7 is real. Screen record the app's home screen at 120 BPM with the moon item active, and composite it onto the phone. Start the recording's beat on a bar marker so the swing matches the click.
  5. Add the gold by hand. The Gnotes, sparks and ring flashes are easier to add as simple overlays in the edit than to get right inside a generated clip, and it keeps them identical from shot to shot.

The beat-exact moments (bootfalls on mushrooms, drum hits) are the hard part for generated video. If a clip drifts, retime it in the edit to the markers; a 5 to 10 percent speed change is invisible.
