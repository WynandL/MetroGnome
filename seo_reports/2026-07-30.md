## Recommendation - ACTIONED TODAY: a rhythm test at /tools/rhythm-timing-test.html

Rather than draft a fifth unsent outreach email, this session shipped the asset. Live now at **https://metrognome.co.za/tools/rhythm-timing-test.html** (commit `5aa5551` on master, Vercel deploy confirmed serving).

### Why this and not another email

The 07-20 GSC pull is unambiguous about which lever works. Impressions went 340 to 1,250 (+268%) and clicks 1 to 7 while **average position stayed flat at 14.5**. Growth is coming from new content creating new matching queries, not from authority. Five outreach threads have produced zero published links in two months. Publishing measurably moves the numbers within about a week (the "Time Signatures Explained" post drove the late-June impressions climb and produced the brand-new `odd meters` query; the Tap Tempo tool plausibly produced `metronome tap`). So: publish, and keep the emails queued.

### Why a rhythm test specifically

It is the rare case where a crowded category has a genuine technical opening, and where the app's existing engine is the answer:

- **The competitors are measuring the wrong thing.** Between a click leaving the speaker and a tap being registered by the page there is a fixed delay from audio output latency, Bluetooth buffering and input handling - a few milliseconds on some setups, well over 100 ms on others. Every existing test scores raw closeness to the beat, so they are partly measuring the visitor's hardware and their scores are not comparable between devices.
- **A constant delay shifts every tap equally, so it leaves the spread between taps untouched.** Scoring the spread is the honest measurement. This is not a repositioning invented for the page - it is verbatim the reasoning in `GrooveScorer.kt` ("the grade is driven by CONSISTENCY, not raw closeness to the click, because... even a perfectly calibrated device leaves a residual latency that shifts every hit by the same amount").
- **It gives the page an editorial hook no competitor can copy without rebuilding their scorer**, and it makes the app the natural next step rather than a bolted-on CTA: the test grades your tapping, Groove Check grades your playing.

### What shipped

- 4-beat count-in plus 16 scored beats at 100 BPM. Beats scheduled on the Web Audio clock with a lookahead window; tap times mapped into the same clock domain via `getOutputTimestamp()` so the comparison itself does not drift.
- Each tap folded onto its nearest beat; taps more than half a beat out are dropped rather than mis-attributed. Minimum 5 counted taps, matching `GrooveScorer.MIN_HITS`.
- Score = spread ramped between **25 ms (full marks) and 110 ms (zero)**, the actual `TIGHT_JITTER_MS` / `LOOSE_JITTER_MS` constants from the app. Spread measured as a scaled median absolute deviation so one stray tap cannot dominate.
- Reports three numbers: spread (drives the score), average offset (shown but explicitly labelled unreliable in a browser), and beats counted.
- Score bands published on-page: Tight 85-100 (under ~38 ms), Solid 65-84, Developing 40-64, Loose 0-39 (over ~76 ms). These are derived from the formula, not invented - verified numerically.
- ~900 words of supporting prose, six-question FAQ, `WebApplication` + `FAQPage` structured data, cross-links both ways with the Tap Tempo tool and the improve-your-timing post.

### Verification done before publishing

The scoring script was extracted and run under a virtual clock with a stubbed DOM and AudioContext, injecting taps with known deviations:

| Scenario | Result |
|---|---|
| Schedule integrity | 20 clicks, 5 accents, gaps 600.000 ms min and max (drift-free) |
| Perfect player | 100, spread 0 ms |
| Tight, +/-10 ms | 100, spread 4 ms |
| **Tight but 120 ms device latency** | **100, spread 8 ms, bias +121 ms** - the central claim holds |
| Loose, +/-90 ms | 55 "Developing", spread 63 ms |
| Consistent 70 ms drag | 100, bias +67 ms, drag called out separately |
| 6 taps only | Scores, with a "sample not a result" caveat |
| 3 taps | Refuses to score, explains why |

One real bug was found and fixed this way: a failed run left the previous run's stats on screen.

## Next up - the blog post to pair with it

`landing/blog/why-you-rush-when-you-play.html` - **"Why You Rush When You Play (And Why It Gets Worse at Slow Tempos)"**

Not written today, deliberately: the tool needs to be the thing that goes out first so the post has something to link to. Spec so it can be written in one sitting:

- **Angle:** rushing and dragging as a diagnosable, measurable habit rather than a character flaw. Opens by having the reader take the rhythm test and read their *average offset* - the number the tool deliberately downplays as a score, but which is exactly the subject of this post.
- **Why it does not cannibalise anything:** `how-to-improve-your-timing.html` is about the feedback loop as a concept; `how-to-practice-increasing-bpm.html` is about speed progression. Neither addresses directional error (rushing vs dragging) or why slow tempos expose it. Confirmed against all 11 existing posts.
- **Substance to cover:** why comfort tempos create the habit; why more BPM hides the error (less room per bar to notice drift); adrenaline and the live-performance rush; separating grooves from fills, which is where drummers specifically accelerate; the bass-player anchor.
- **Competition:** Drummer Cafe, Sonicbids and Soundbrenner all have posts on this. The differentiator is that this one starts with the reader's own measured number instead of general advice, which is a link the others cannot make.
- **Cross-links:** rhythm test tool (primary), improve-your-timing, metronome-app-for-drummers.
