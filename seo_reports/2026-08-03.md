**Trend research (today's specific ask) - one genuinely new, large finding:**

**"Say the Word on Beat" is a real, large, currently-live viral trend, and it sits directly on top of Metro Gnome's existing rhythm-test positioning.** It's a short-video game (images or words flash to a beat, name each one before the next one appears, gets faster every round) that exploded on TikTok in late 2025 and has since spread to Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts, with individual clips in the millions of plays and at least two dedicated apps on Google Play plus a standalone website (saythewordonbeat.com) built around it. It is still actively growing as of August 2026, not a trend that already peaked. Two things make this directly useful rather than just interesting:

1. It is, mechanically, a subdivision-and-anticipation test, not a reaction-time test - exactly the skill the rhythm test tool (shipped 07-30) and Groove Check are built to measure and train. The connection is not a stretch invented for SEO purposes; it is the actual underlying mechanism.
2. None of the game's coverage or competing sites explain *why* it's hard in those terms, and none of them offer an actual millisecond measurement the way the rhythm test does. That's a genuine content gap, not a crowded angle.

## Recommendation - ACTIONED TODAY: "Say the Word on Beat" trend post, shipped and cross-linked

Rather than write this up as a proposal, it's live now at **https://metrognome.co.za/blog/say-the-word-on-beat-rhythm-skill.html** (commit `4bd7a50` on master, deploy verified serving).

### Why build instead of describe

Same logic as 07-30: five outreach threads have produced zero published links in two months, while publishing content has moved impressions and clicks every time it's been tried. A live, growing trend with a genuine, non-invented connection to the app's own positioning is a rare enough opening that shipping it same-day beats queuing it.

### What shipped

- **"The Real Skill Behind 'Say the Word on Beat' (And How to Actually Get Better)"** - ~1,600 words. Explains the game is testing prediction/subdivision, not reaction time; explains why musicians tend to win at it (metronome practice trains the identical skill); explains why the game itself can't actually measure your rhythm (no directional detail, uncontrolled device latency); points the reader at the existing rhythm test for a real number; gives concrete practice steps; bridges to Groove Check for anyone who plays an instrument.
- Article + FAQPage structured data (5 questions), both validated to parse.
- No claim of affiliation with the actual game or its trademark anywhere in the piece; it's framed throughout as independent commentary and analysis, which is both accurate and the safer legal posture.
- Cross-linked in both directions: from the rhythm test tool page (new lead entry in "Also useful"), from the rush/drag post (new entry in "Also on the blog"), from the blog index (new lead card), and from the home page's blog teaser (now leads, "Meet Metro" rotated out of the two-slot teaser following the same pattern used on 07-30).
- Sitemap updated: new URL added, home and `/blog/` lastmod bumped to today.
- Zero em/en dashes sitewide, re-swept after this change. All internal links resolve. Both JSON-LD blocks parse.

### Keywords targeted

`say the word on beat`, `rhythm challenge`, `am I good at rhythm`, `test your rhythm`, `how to get better at rhythm games` - checked against all 13 existing posts plus the rhythm test tool, no cannibalisation (the rhythm test owns the "measure me" angle, this post owns the trend-explainer angle and funnels into the test rather than duplicating it).
