What this pitch test measures

Every round plays two short notes, one after the other. One of them is a little higher than the other, and your only job is to say which way the second note went. Get two rounds in a row right and the next pair is closer together. Miss one and the gap opens up again. Over 24 rounds the gap settles around the smallest difference you can reliably hear, and that is your result.

This is called pitch discrimination, and it is the finest-grained part of relative pitch. It is what lets a guitarist hear that a string is slightly flat before looking at a tuner, a singer find the centre of a note, and a string player put a finger exactly where the note lives.

What a cent is

Pitch differences this small are measured in cents. A cent is one hundredth of a semitone, and a semitone is the step between two neighbouring keys on a piano. So a result of 25 cents means you can hear a difference a quarter the size of the smallest step on a keyboard, and a result of 5 cents means one twentieth of it. It is the same unit your tuner's needle shows, which is why tuner accuracy is quoted in cents too.

The starting note changes every round. If every pair started on the same note, you could ignore the first one and just remember where the second one usually sits. Moving the pair around makes every round a fresh comparison, so the result measures your ear rather than your memory of the test.

What your result means

Lower is finer. The bands below are a guide to where your ear is today, not a ceiling. One round played on laptop speakers in a noisy room can easily read twice as wide as the same ear on headphones.

Yes, your ear can get sharper

Fine pitch hearing is a trained skill rather than a fixed gift. In a 2006 study, Christophe Micheyl and colleagues measured listeners with no musical training and found their thresholds started about six times wider than those of classical musicians. After roughly four to eight hours of practice spread over sessions, the untrained group had caught up with the musicians.

Three habits help most:

  • Practise little and often. Two or three runs of this test a day will do more than one long session a week.
  • Tune by ear first, then check. Tune a string against a reference by ear, then look at the tuner to see how close you got. The check after the guess is the part that trains you.
  • Hold notes against a drone. Playing a long note over a steady reference tone makes small pitch errors audible as a slow wobble called beating. The free tuning drone does this in your browser, and the intonation practice guide explains the routine.

Questions about pitch tests

What is a cent?
A cent is one hundredth of a semitone. The gap between two neighbouring keys on a piano is 100 cents, so a result of 10 cents means you can hear a difference one tenth the size of the smallest step on a keyboard.
What is a good pitch test score?
Lower is finer. Under 5 cents is a finely tuned ear, 5 to 12 cents is sharp, and 12 to 25 cents is a good, musical ear. Results above that are common on a first try, especially on laptop speakers, and they come down quickly with practice.
Can you train your ear to hear smaller pitch differences?
Yes, and faster than most people expect. In a 2006 study by Micheyl and colleagues, listeners without musical training started with pitch thresholds about six times larger than classical musicians, and reached the musicians' level after roughly four to eight hours of practice.
Is this the same as a perfect pitch test?
No. Perfect pitch is naming a note with no reference. This test measures relative pitch at its finest: how small a difference between two notes you can hear. That is the skill that lets you tune by ear and play in tune with others, and everyone can improve it.
Why did I get a different result the second time?
Some variation is normal. A lucky or unlucky guess on a close pair can move the result a little. Run it three times and take the best: that is closer to what your ear can do than any single run.
Is this pitch test free?
Yes. It runs entirely in your browser, needs no download, account or payment, and nothing you answer is uploaded anywhere.
