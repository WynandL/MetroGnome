How a speed trainer works

Building speed on a passage is a ladder. You play a few bars at one tempo, move the metronome up a little, and play them again, until the tempo you want feels normal. The trouble with an ordinary metronome is that every rung means stopping, reaching over and changing the number. A speed trainer climbs the ladder for you: you choose where it starts, where it ends, how big each rung is and how many bars to spend on each one, and your hands never leave the instrument.

The ladder above the settings shows every tempo the session will visit, and it updates as you change them. The gold bar fills across each step, so you always know how long you have before the next jump.

Why come back down

Choose Up and back down and the trainer walks down through the same tempos once it reaches the target. Speed hides unevenness that slower tempos reveal, so the trip down checks that your control held at every rung, and the session ends at a tempo you can play cleanly rather than on the edge of what you can manage. Note the step where things first went wrong on the way up: that tempo, not the target, is where the next session's work is.

Speed is only useful if it stays even. Once you can play at the target, check that the tempo holds when the click goes quiet with the steady tempo test, and that each note lands where it should with the rhythm test.

Questions about speed trainers

What is a speed trainer metronome?
A metronome that changes its own tempo while you play. You set where to start, where to finish, how big each step is and how many bars to play at each tempo, and it moves up a step every time those bars are done, so your hands stay on the instrument.
How much should I increase the BPM each step?
5 BPM suits most material, with 4 bars at each tempo. Use smaller steps for fast or technical passages and 8 bars when you want more repetitions before each jump. Steps of 20 BPM or more skip the tempos where problems first appear.
Why come back down after reaching the target?
Slower tempos expose unevenness that speed hides, and stepping down through the same tempos checks that control held all the way up. It also ends the session at a tempo you can play cleanly.
Is this speed trainer free?
Yes. It runs entirely in your browser, needs no download, account or payment, and remembers your settings on this device only.
