7/4 Time Signature: How to Count It, How It Differs From 7/8, and a Click to Practise With

7/4 time has seven quarter-note beats in every bar, slow enough that each beat is felt on its own, which is why the easiest way into 7/4 is to hear it as a bar of 4/4 followed by a bar of 3/4. That 4+3 shape is the verse riff of Pink Floyd's Money and the groove of Solsbury Hill. What 7/4 time means

Seven beats per bar, with the quarter note carrying the beat. Seven does not split into equal twos or threes, so 7/4 is an odd meter, and like 7/8 it is built from twos and threes. The difference from 7/8 is the beat value: in 7/4 each of the seven beats is slow enough to feel individually, so the bar is long, and most 7/4 music is felt as a bar of four plus a bar of three (4+3) or three plus four (3+4) rather than as three quick groups. The tool above defaults to 2+2+3, the same shape as 4+3 with the four subdivided; in the Metro Gnome app you can set the accents to beats 1 and 5 only for a pure 4+3 feel.

Rock and pop lean heavily on 4+3 because it lets a familiar four-beat groove run and then turns the bar around one beat early. That early turnaround is the hook of every famous 7/4 riff.

How to count 7/4

4+3: count ONE two three four ONE two three, accents on beats 1 and 5. A bar of four and a bar of three, the Money feel.

3+4: count ONE two three ONE two three four, accents on beats 1 and 4. The short group first; less common but just as natural.

2+2+3: count ONE two ONE two ONE two three, accents on 1, 3 and 5. The tool's default, useful when the piece moves in pairs.

At a slow tempo you can count all seven; that is what makes 7/4 friendlier than 7/8. But the grouping still has to be there, or seven even beats will drift into 4/4 and you will lose a beat every bar.

What 7/4 feels like

7/4 feels like a groove that turns the corner early. For four beats it is a rock song; then the bar ends a beat before the ear expects, and the downbeat of the next bar arrives with a jolt. At slow tempos that jolt is a swagger (Money); at faster ones it is a skip (Solsbury Hill). Because the beats are slow, 7/4 never feels frantic the way 7/8 can; it feels lopsided and confident.

Songs and pieces in 7/4

  • Money (Pink Floyd): the bass riff and verses are in 7/4, felt as 4+3, before the guitar solo drops into 4/4.
  • Solsbury Hill (Peter Gabriel): a hit single in 7/4 that most listeners never notice is in seven, which is the point.
  • Unsquare Dance (The Dave Brubeck Quartet): 7/4 with hand claps marking the grouping, written as a deliberate puzzle for the listener.
  • Outshined (Soundgarden): the main riff sits in 7/4, one of the best-known odd-meter grooves in grunge.

How to practise 7/4 with a metronome

Loop a bar of 4/4 and a bar of 3/4. Set the tool to 4/4, play a bar, then 3/4, play a bar, and imagine them joined. Once the turnaround feels natural, set 7/4 and the bar is already in your hands.

Anchor beat 5. In 4+3 the accent on beat 5 is where players lose the bar. Clap beats 1 and 5 with the click until beat 5 feels like a second downbeat, then add the instrument.

Learn the songs. Playing along to Money or Solsbury Hill with the tool at the same tempo is the fastest way to internalise 7/4, because the groove does the counting for you.

Questions about 7/4

What is the difference between 7/4 and 7/8?
Both have seven beats grouped in twos and threes, but in 7/4 the beat is a quarter note and in 7/8 it is an eighth note. 7/4 is used for slower sevens where each beat is felt individually, typically as 4+3; 7/8 is used for fast sevens felt as three quick groups like 2+2+3.
How do you count 7/4 time?
Most often as a bar of four plus a bar of three: ONE two three four ONE two three, with accents on beats 1 and 5. Other pieces use 3+4 (accents on 1 and 4) or 2+2+3 (accents on 1, 3 and 5). At a slow tempo you can count all seven, but keep the grouping or the bar will drift into 4/4.
What songs are in 7/4?
Pink Floyd's Money (verses and bass riff), Peter Gabriel's Solsbury Hill, Dave Brubeck's Unsquare Dance and Soundgarden's Outshined are the best-known examples, all felt as a four followed by a three.
