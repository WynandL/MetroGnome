Which tool for which goal?

Each tool measures or trains one thing. If you are not sure where to start, pick the goal closest to yours.

"My timing is off, but I am not sure how"

Start with the rhythm test. It tells you two separate things: your average offset (whether you sit ahead of the click or behind it) and your spread (how much your taps scatter). They need different fixes. A consistent offset is a habit you can correct by listening; a wide spread means the beat itself is not yet steady, which slow practice with subdivisions fixes. Then try the steady tempo test, which takes the click away and shows whether you speed up or slow down on your own.

"I can play it slowly but not up to speed"

Use the speed trainer. Set the tempo you can play cleanly as the start and the performance tempo as the target, and let it climb a few BPM every few bars. If it falls apart at a step, the step was too big. The reasoning is in how to practise increasing BPM.

"I need a click for practice"

The online metronome covers everyday practice, with tap tempo and accents. For a meter like 7/8 or 11/8, the odd meter metronome shows the count group by group, and time signatures explained charts the common meters. To find the tempo of a recording, use tap tempo.

"I want to play more in tune"

Hold a note on the tuning drone and play scales or long tones against it: when you are out of tune you hear a slow wobble (beating) that stops when you land on the pitch. Singers in Hindustani and Carnatic music can use the shruti box preset to their Sa. The pitch test shows how small a pitch difference you can hear, which is the limit of what you can correct by ear.

"I want to understand the music I play"

The chord finder names any chord you tap on a guitar or piano and spells its notes, which helps when a chart lists a chord you do not know or a shape you found has no name. The guide to how chords work explains the naming.

"I play with both hands, or on a kit"

The polyrhythm test scores 3 against 2, 4 against 3 and 5 against 4, and shows which hand drifts.

"I teach"

All of these work in a lesson on a laptop or a phone, and most can be embedded on a school or studio page. The tests give students a number they can try to beat next week, and the rhythm test result is a useful starting point for a conversation about rushing and dragging.
