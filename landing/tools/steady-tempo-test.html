What this tempo test measures

A metronome is a crutch in the best sense: while it clicks, it quietly corrects you on every beat. This test takes the crutch away. You get four beats to hear the tempo and eight to lock in with it, and then the click goes silent and you keep tapping for sixteen beats on your own. Everything you tap in that silence is the sound of your inner clock.

At the end you get three numbers. Tempo you held is the typical tempo of your silent beats. Start to finish follows a line drawn through all your silent beats, so it shows whether you gradually sped up or slowed down, and by how much. Tap to tap is how far individual beats strayed from that line, as a percentage of the tempo. Your steady score combines how close you stayed to the given tempo, drift included, with how even your beats were.

Device delay cannot touch this score. The result is built only from the gaps between your own taps after the click stops. Speaker, headphone and Bluetooth delays shift every tap by the same amount, which leaves those gaps exactly as they were, so this test is as fair on a laptop as on a studio setup.

Three ways to steady your inner clock

Count the space between beats. Drift lives in the gaps. Count "one and two and" or "one e and a" in your head, and the gaps stop being empty time you have to guess across. This matters most at slow tempos, which is why rushing gets worse at slow tempos.

Practise with the click switching off. Play along with a metronome for a few bars, then mute it for a few bars and listen for whether you land back on it when it returns. It is this test, turned into a daily habit.

Measure your timing as well as your tempo. Holding a tempo and landing each beat cleanly are related but different skills. Try the rhythm test for the second one, and how to improve your timing for why measuring the result is what makes practice work.

Questions about tempo tests

What does the steady tempo test measure?
How well you keep a tempo once the click is gone. It compares the tempo you held during sixteen silent beats with the tempo you were given, checks whether you sped up or slowed down, and measures how even your taps were from one to the next.
Does my device's audio delay affect the result?
No. The score is built only from the time between your own taps during the silent part. A delay in your speakers or headphones shifts every tap by the same amount, so the gaps between them are unchanged.
Why do I speed up when the click stops?
Most players drift faster without a reference, especially at slow tempos where the gaps between beats are long and the urge to fill them is strong. Counting the subdivisions between beats is the most reliable fix.
Is this tempo test free?
Yes. It runs entirely in your browser, needs no download, account or payment, and nothing you tap is uploaded anywhere.
