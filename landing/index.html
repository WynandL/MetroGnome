How the tools are made

Metro Gnome is built by Wynand Lambrechts, an electronic engineer. A metronome is a timing problem and a tuner is a pitch problem, and both can be measured, so nothing goes live on a hunch. The music theory is checked with the professional musicians he consults.

  • Clicks are scheduled on the audio clock, so they do not drift.
  • Taps are timed from the moment your finger lands, not when it lifts.
  • Scored tests are run against simulated players of known ability before release.
  • Chord names and theory are checked with professional musicians and against references, with over two hundred test cases behind the chord finder.

Questions

Are the tools on this site free?
Yes. Every tool on metrognome.co.za runs in your browser with no download, account or payment, on a computer, tablet or phone. Some pages carry a small number of ads, never inside a tool.
Which test should I start with?
The rhythm test. It takes about twenty seconds, measures in milliseconds how close to the click you tap, and tells you whether you tend to rush or drag. From there, the steady tempo test checks whether you can hold the beat without the click, and the rushing or dragging test checks how small a tempo change you can hear.
Do the tools work on a phone?
Yes. They work in any modern mobile browser. On an iPhone or iPad, web audio can be silenced by the ring/silent switch, so if a tool shows movement but makes no sound, switch to ring. Tapping tests are scored from the moment your finger lands, so they work on a touch screen as well as with a keyboard.
Can I use the tools in lessons or on my own website?
Yes. Teachers are welcome to use them in lessons, and most tools can be embedded on another site with one line of HTML, free, with no sign up. The embed page has the code.
Is there an app?
Yes, for Android. Metro Gnome is a free app with a metronome, a chromatic tuner that copes with noisy rooms, a tuning drone, a chord finder, a rhythm game and a speed trainer. Optional one-time purchases remove ads or add extra click sounds, drone voices and an item for Metro; there is no subscription. The browser tools on this site are free whether or not you install it.
Who makes Metro Gnome?
Wynand Lambrechts, an electronic engineer, builds the tools and writes the guides, and checks the music theory with the professional musicians he consults. Tools that also exist in the Android app (the metronome, odd meters, chord finder, drone and tap tempo) use the same rules as the app, so both give the same answers, and scored tests are checked against simulated players of known ability before they go live.
